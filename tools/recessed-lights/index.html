    CEILING LAYOUT GUIDE

    Create a measured recessed-light ceiling plan

    Recessed lighting is a two-dimensional layout problem. The room length, room width, number of rows, lights per row, wall offsets, and fixture diameter all affect how the finished ceiling reads. This calculator converts those inputs into exact X/Y center coordinates for every fixture.

    Balanced versus fixed wall offsets

    Balanced mode distributes the first and last light centers evenly from the walls based on the selected row and column counts. Fixed-offset mode lets you specify the distance from the perimeter to the first and last light centers. The blueprint shows row spacing, column spacing, wall offsets, and every fixture coordinate.

    Example: A 16×12-foot room with two rows of four 6-inch fixtures can be mapped into eight exact ceiling centers. Change to a 4-inch fixture and the center coordinates remain the same, but the blueprint now shows the smaller fixture diameter at room scale.

    Planning considerations

    • Use fixture center locations for layout, not the outside diameter of the trim.
    • Check joists, ducts, plumbing, wiring, fans, and other ceiling conflicts before cutting.
    • Fixture diameter affects the physical footprint, even when it does not change center spacing.
    • Lighting quantity and spacing should also consider lumen output, beam angle, ceiling height, and the room’s use.

    Does this calculate how many lights I need?

    No. You choose the row and column count; the tool focuses on producing a clean geometric layout.

    Why are X/Y coordinates useful?

    They let you locate every center from two fixed room edges without accumulating spacing error from one fixture to the next.
